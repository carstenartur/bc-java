## Aggregate workload results and the negative control

Ratios are equal-field geometric means of optimized/baseline; below 1 means less elapsed time.

| JVM | Constructor | Construct plus one ratio | Reuse, unchanged method |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 21 | 0.540633 | 0.703704 | 0.963367 |
| 25 | 0.530517 | 0.710234 | 1.000505 |

Thus the constructor uses 45.94% less time on the Java 21 runner and 46.95% less on the Java 25 runner. Construction plus one ratio uses 29.63% and 28.98% less time, respectively; all eight of those intervals are also separated.

**Do not claim a reuse speedup.** The `sqrtRatio` method is unchanged. The Java 25 reuse control is essentially neutral (ratio 1.000505; all intervals overlap). Java 21 shows an apparent 3.66% aggregate difference, with three non-overlapping field intervals despite unchanged computational code. This is a warning about benchmark/run/JIT effects; its cause has not been isolated. It does not explain a genuine new steady-state optimization. Follow-up repetitions must keep all fields and report this control rather than selecting favorable rows. The modest control anomaly also cautions against treating the constructor percentage as a universally exact effect.

This is evidence for faster initialization of this calculator, not faster full hash-to-curve operations, RSA key generation, an entire Bouncy Castle application, or stronger cryptographic security. The rewrite was supplied by the assistant; autonomous Regelsuche discovery is not established here.
